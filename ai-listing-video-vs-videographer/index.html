AI listing video vs a videographer

There are now three ways to get a video for a listing. An app that animates your photos for a few dollars, a videographer who comes to the property for a few hundred, or a film built from your photos by a person. They are not the same product, and the price tells you which one you are getting.

What the apps get wrong

The apps animate one photo at a time. Each still gets a few seconds of push in or pan, then a cut to the next. The newer ones use a video model to add camera motion, and that is where the trouble starts. Trade press has a name for it now, AI slop. Counters warp, cabinet edges wobble, rooms bend at the corners. A buyer notices before you do, and a video that looks off does more harm to a listing than no video.

What the videographer gets right, and what it costs

A real shoot is the best possible result. It is also $300 to $800 a property, needs scheduling, needs the place staged and empty for half a day, and shows the property on one day in one light. For a vacation rental that turns over weekly, most owners never get around to it.

Where we sit

We build the film from the photos already on your listing, so there is nothing to schedule. The camera moves through the rooms in the order a guest would walk them, with a color grade and a music bed. Then a person watches every frame and fixes anything that bent. You see it before you pay, and changes are free until you are happy. It is the result of a shoot at about a quarter of the price, from photos you already own.
